About the certificate

DELE is the official Spanish language diploma awarded by the Instituto Cervantes on behalf of Spain’s Ministry of Education. It spans the CEFR scale and, like DELF, does not expire.

Anyone who needs Spanish on paper — for study, for work, or as part of a residency or nationality application — rather than a score that lapses after two years.

For exam dates, fees, formats and registration, go to the Instituto Cervantes. Those details change by country and year, so this page does not restate them.

The scale

What each Spanish level means.

DELE certificates are graded on the CEFR, the same six-level scale used across European language exams. Here is what the levels covered by these apps actually describe.

A1Breakthrough
You can handle everyday phrases and very basic expressions: introducing yourself, saying where you live, asking simple questions about familiar things. Conversation works when the other person speaks slowly and helps you along.
A2Waystage
You can deal with routine situations — shopping, appointments, directions, work basics — and describe your background and immediate surroundings in simple terms. Exchanges are short but no longer need much help.
B1Threshold
You can cope with most situations that come up while travelling, follow the main points of clear standard speech on familiar topics, and produce connected text about things that interest you. This is the level most residency and work requirements ask for.
